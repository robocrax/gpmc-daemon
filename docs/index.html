Every photo you take, in Google Photos at original quality.

GPMC is a free app for your own computer. Your phone sends it new photos, and it uploads them to your Google Photos account. No GPMC service sits in the middle.

    Set it up once, then just take photos.

    You need a Windows PC, a Google account and a Syncthing app on your phone. Everything runs on your own devices.

    1. Install GPMC on your computer

      Download the installer and run it. GPMC opens in your browser, then waits in the tray by the clock and starts again each time you sign in. Syncthing comes built in.

      Download for Windows Or get the portable zip

      For Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit. The app isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may say it doesn't recognise it. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

      Have a home server that never sleeps?

      Run GPMC there instead, and backups carry on while your PC is off. Pick whichever fits, then open the address it prints (port 8080) in a browser.

      curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robocrax/gpmc-daemon/main/deploy/proxmox-deploy.sh | bash
      docker run -d --name gpmc-daemon --restart unless-stopped \
  -p 8080:8080 \
  -v ./data/config:/config \
  -v ./data/sync:/sync \
  robocrax/gpmc-daemon:latest
      git clone https://github.com/robocrax/gpmc-daemon && cd gpmc-daemon
sudo ./install.sh

      Run the Proxmox command on the Proxmox host. It creates a small container and installs Syncthing in it too. With Docker or the Debian installer, install Syncthing on the same machine yourself.

    2. Connect your Google account

      Choose Connect and sign in to Google. You copy one sign-in token from your browser and paste it into GPMC, and the app shows you where to find it.

      GPMC keeps the upload credential on your computer. It never sees your Google password.

    3. Link your phone

      Install a Syncthing app, then open Device Sync in GPMC. Scan the code it shows to add your computer, and choose which Google account this phone backs up to.

      iPhone or iPad
      Get Synctrain on the App Store. It is free, and its photo backup fills the shared folder from your camera roll.
      Android
      Any Syncthing app works, Syncthing-Fork included. Syncthing keeps a list of Android apps.

      When the shared folder appears on the phone, accept it, point it at your camera folder and set it to Send Only. New photos start arriving on their own.

    Questions people ask first.

    Do my photos go through your servers?

    No. There is no GPMC service and no account to create. Photos go from your phone to your own computer, encrypted end to end by Syncthing, and from there to your Google account.

    Will it delete photos from my phone?

    No. Your phone only sends and your computer only receives. When GPMC clears an uploaded file from your computer, that change stays on your computer.

    Does my computer need to stay on?

    Only to upload. While it is off or asleep, new photos wait on your phone and go across the next time both are on. For backups around the clock, run GPMC on a home server.

    Is this made by Google?

    No. GPMC is an unofficial tool. It uploads through the same interface the Google Photos Android app uses, by way of the gpmc library. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google, so use it only with accounts you own.

    Does GPMC store my Google password?

    No. You sign in on Google's own page. GPMC stores the upload credential that sign-in produces, in a small database on your computer.

    How soon does a new photo show up in Google Photos?

    GPMC checks every five minutes unless you change the interval in Settings. Use Back up now to start a run straight away.

    What can it run on?

    A Windows 10 or 11 PC, or a home server running Proxmox, Docker, Debian or Ubuntu. Either way you use it from your web browser.

    GPMC is free, and its code is on GitHub. The upload engine is xob0t/gpmc, and the sign-in flow follows gotohp.
